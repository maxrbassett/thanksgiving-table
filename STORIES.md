# The adventures, one by one

What the player does in each story, the facts it shows, and the people it thanks.
The facts and thank-you lists below are copied from the game itself.
Every adventure keeps words short: a goal of a few words at the top of the screen, short speech bubbles, and about a dozen one-line “In real life” notes that pop up while you play.
Most steps are “walk up to the thing and press E”; a few of them open a little game that matches what is being done right then (listed under each adventure; the ones marked *(new)* were added in the fourth round).

## 🦃 The Turkey

*Adventure: How does the turkey end up on your table?*

Source: your document “Thanksgiving Dinner – The Turkey” (its six sections, in order).

**What you do**

1. **Building a place to grow** — mark the four corners with the surveyor, pour the concrete, hammer up the walls (the roof drops on), switch on water and power.
2. **The hatchery** — carry eggs into the incubator, turn the racks, keep warmth and humidity in the green; the poults hatch and follow you to the delivery truck.
3. **Feeding and caring** — drive the combine through the corn, grind feed at the mill, fill the feeders, turn on water and fans; months later the poults are big gobbling turkeys and you help the vet with a poorly one.
4. **The processing plant** — herd the turkeys to the truck, wash your hands, stamp the inspected turkeys as they pass. (Nothing of the slaughter is shown; the inspector says “Crews in there do the hardest job”.)
5. **Chill, pack and ship** — freeze, weigh and label a turkey, drive the forklift, ride the refrigerated truck to town, take your turkey from the store freezer and pay.
6. **Home kitchen** — thaw, season, roast, and carry the golden turkey to the table.

**Little games along the way** (you play them with the keys, the mouse or a touch screen; a wrong try only nudges you, and each one always ends in success)

- 🌡️ **Keep the incubator warm** — hold the gauge in the green (the hatchery).
- 🧠 **Mix the feed** *(new)* — a recipe card shows three scoops (corn, soy, vitamins), then hides them; tap the scoops into the mixer in the same order. Second batch: four scoops. The candy is a trap (the feed mill).
- ✅ **Stamp the inspected turkeys** — press at the right moment as each one passes (the processing plant).
- 🦃 **Herd the turkeys** to the truck (the processing plant).
- 🏷️ **Match the label** *(new)* — read the needle on the scale dial and tap the label with the same weight (pack and ship).
- 🌡️ **Is it done?** *(new)* — hold the thermometer button and let go right at 165 °F, the safe temperature for turkey (the home kitchen).

**“In real life” one-liners** (in the order they can appear)

- 📐 Engineers plan where rain will drain before crews move earth.
- 🌬️ Installers fit barns with heating, ventilation, feeders and controls.
- 🐦 Long before hatching, breeding specialists select the parent birds.
- 🥚 Eggs need warmth, humidity and turning for about four weeks.
- 🐣 Baby turkeys are called poults.
- 🌽 Farmers grow corn and soybeans for turkey feed.
- ⚖️ Nutritionists plan a balanced diet, and feed-mill workers prepare it.
- 🦃 Turkeys need daily care for months before Thanksgiving.
- 🚚 Loading crews and drivers bring ready birds to the processing plant.
- 🔍 Inspectors and food-safety teams check the turkeys and the plant.
- 🌡️ Workers chill or freeze each turkey so it travels safely.
- 📋 Dispatchers coordinate the deliveries; refrigerated-truck drivers carry them.
- ❄️ One turkey in a freezer took many coordinated efforts.
- 🗓️ Someone planned this meal and allowed time for thawing.

**People thanked**

- 🏗️ Surveyors, Concrete workers, Carpenters, Electricians, Plumbers
- 🥚 Breeding specialists, Egg collectors, Incubator technicians, Poult-delivery drivers
- 🌽 Corn and soybean farmers, Grain haulers, Animal nutritionists, Feed-mill operators
- 🦃 Turkey growers, Barn workers, Veterinarians
- 🔍 Loading crews, Live-haul drivers, Processing crews, USDA inspectors, Sanitation crews
- ❄️ Refrigeration technicians, Scale operators, Packaging workers, Label printers
- 🚚 Forklift operators, Dispatchers, Refrigerated-truck drivers, Stockers, Cashiers
- 🍽️ Shoppers, Cooks, Carvers, Platter carriers, Dish washers

Closing line: “Many hands, one golden turkey — thank you, every one!”

## 🥔 Mashed Potatoes

*Adventure: Where do mashed potatoes begin?*

No document from you for this one; you named it in your message (“a potato farm where you get to farm potatoes”). Facts are kept simple.

**What you do**

1. **The potato field** — plant seed potatoes, turn on the sprinklers, shoo the beetles, dig up plants by hand, then drive the harvester through the rows (a farm dog follows you).
2. **The packing shed** — wash the potatoes, toss out the rocks, fill and sew a sack, load the truck.
3. **The market** — fill a bag, weigh it, pay.
4. **Home kitchen** — peel, chop, boil, mash, add butter and milk.

**Little games along the way** (you play them with the keys, the mouse or a touch screen; a wrong try only nudges you, and each one always ends in success)

- 🌱 **Tap the sprouted one** *(new)* — three potatoes, one with green sprouts at its eyes: that is the seed potato. Three rounds (the field).
- 🐞 **Shoo the beetles** (the field).
- 🚜 **Drive the harvester** through the rows (the field).
- 🪨 **Toss out the rocks** (the packing shed).
- ➰ **Stitch on the dot!** *(new)* — a needle glides along the top of the sack; press when it is over the glowing dot, five stitches close it (the packing shed).
- 🥛 **Hold to pour, stop in green** *(new)* — pour milk into the measuring cup and let go while it is in the green band (the home kitchen).

**“In real life” one-liners** (in the order they can appear)

- 👀 The “eyes” on a seed potato sprout into new plants.
- 🌱 Farmers let seed potatoes sprout first — it is called chitting.
- 💦 A potato is mostly water, so the plants drink a lot.
- 🌸 Potato plants bloom on top — the potatoes hide below.
- 🍂 Dry tops mean the potatoes below have finished growing.
- 🥔 One seed potato grew a whole bunch of new ones!
- 🚜 A harvester lifts potatoes and shakes off the soil.
- 🌙 Potatoes can wait months in cool, dark sheds before washing.
- 🕶️ Sacks keep out light — sunlight turns potatoes green.
- 🌈 There are thousands of kinds of potatoes — even purple ones!
- ⏱️ Small pieces cook faster than whole potatoes.
- 🍴 They are ready when a fork slides in easily.
- 🥛 Add milk a little at a time — too much makes soup!

**People thanked**

- 🌱 Seed-potato growers, Farmers, Soil scientists
- 💧 Irrigation crews, Well drillers, Weather forecasters
- 🚜 Farm workers, Harvester drivers, Tractor mechanics
- 🏭 Washers, Sorters, Packers, Machine builders
- 🚚 Truck drivers, Warehouse crews, Stockers
- 🛒 Cashiers, Grocers, The shopper in your family
- 🥛 Dairy farmers, Home cooks, Dish washers

Closing line: “From a seed potato to a fluffy spoonful — look who helped!”

## 🥫 Cranberry Sauce

*Adventure: How do cranberries become sauce?*

Source: your document “Thanksgiving Dinner – Cranberry Sauce” (its ten stages merged into six chapters, same order).

**What you do**

1. **The bog** — plant vine cuttings and watch them weave a carpet; four years later lead bumblebees to the blossoms.
2. **Frost night and winter** — turn on the sprinklers so ice protects the buds; ride the sander across the ice.
3. **The red lake** — flood the bed, push the “egg beater” reel so the berries float up, pull the boom to corral them, pump them into the truck.
4. **The bounce test** — a helicopter brings a bin of dry-picked berries; sort bouncy berries from soft ones.
5. **The market** — find juice, dried berries, jam and sauce; buy fresh berries.
6. **Home kitchen** — pour, stir until the berries pop, press through the sieve, chill into a wobbly jelly.

**Little games along the way** (you play them with the keys, the mouse or a touch screen; a wrong try only nudges you, and each one always ends in success)

- 🐝 **Lead the bees** to the flowers (the bog).
- 🌡️ **Frost watch** *(new)* — the night gets colder and the red column falls; tap “Sprinklers on!” the moment it is in the blue band. Too soon: keep watching; too late: frost, try again (frost night).
- 🔴 **The bounce test** — sort the good berries from the bad (the sorting house).
- 🔥 **Simmer the berries** *(new)* — hold for heat and keep the needle in the green SIMMER band until eight berries pop; too hot and the pot foams over (the home kitchen).

**“In real life” one-liners** (in the order they can appear)

- 🌿 Cranberries grow on vines in sandy soil — not under water!
- 🕰️ Some beds still grow vines planted over 100 years ago!
- 🌸 One acre can hold about 20 million blossoms!
- 🥁 Some bees drum on the flower with their legs!
- ⏰ Frost alarms wake growers at night to start the sprinklers!
- ❄️ As water freezes, it gives off heat. That protects the berries!
- ⛄ When the ice melts, the sand settles onto the vines.
- 🌊 Growers build each bed so water can flow in and drain out.
- 🔴 Each berry has four air pockets. That's why it floats!
- 🚁 Some Massachusetts growers fly berry bins by helicopter!
- 📦 Berries sold fresh are combed from dry vines — no flooding!
- 🍹 Juice makers can take the water out, then add it back later!
- 🥄 Bursting berries let out pectin. It makes the sauce thick!

**People thanked**

- 🌱 Soil specialists, Drainage crews, Vine suppliers, Planting crews
- 🐝 Beekeepers, Pollination researchers, Growers
- ❄️ Growers on cold nights, Irrigation technicians, Mechanics, Weather specialists, Winter crews
- 🌊 Water crews, Reel drivers, Boom crews, Pump operators, Truck drivers
- 🚁 Dry-harvest crews, Grading workers, Helicopter pilots, Fresh-fruit packers
- 🏭 Juice makers, Jam makers, Drying crews, Can makers
- 🛒 Forklift operators, Truck drivers, Stockers, Cashiers
- 🥄 Recipe developers, Food scientists, Quality checkers, Home cooks

Closing line: “From a sandy bog to a ruby-red spoonful — look who helped!”

## 🥧 Pumpkin Pie

No document from you; pumpkin pie is an item I added. Facts are kept simple and number-free.

3. **Wheat for the crust** — drive the combine, pour grain into the mill, collect the flour.
4. **The barnyard** — gather eggs among the hens, milk the cow, churn butter.
5. **Spice island** — peel cinnamon bark, pick nutmeg and cloves, dig ginger, cut sugar cane, carry a sack aboard the ship.
6. **Home kitchen** — roll the crust, fill the bowl, whisk, bake, cool, add whipped cream.

**Little games along the way** (you play them with the keys, the mouse or a touch screen; a wrong try only nudges you, and each one always ends in success)

- 🚜 **Drive the combine** (the wheat field).
- 🥫 **Fill the cans** — stop at the right moment (the cannery).
- 🐄 **Left, right, left, right!** *(new)* — milk the cow by hand: squeeze with the glowing hand, alternating left and right, until the pail is full (the barnyard).
- 📦 **Catch the sacks!** *(new)* — the dock crew tosses spice sacks up to the deck; run under the shadow to catch each one (Spice Island).
- 🥖 **Hold to roll. Stop in green!** *(new)* — roll the crust until it fills the pie dish ring; too far and it tears and springs back (the home kitchen).

**“In real life” one-liners** (in the order they can appear)

- 🍂 Pumpkins are canned at harvest time — enough for the whole year.

- 🚜 A combine cuts wheat and shakes out the grain, all at once.
- 🌾 Flour is simply wheat seeds, crushed into a fine powder.
- 🐔 A good laying hen lays an egg almost every day.
- 🥛 Churn cream long enough and it turns into butter!
- 🌳 Cinnamon is the dried inner bark of a tree!
- 🌰 Nutmeg is a seed hiding inside a fruit.
- 🌱 Ginger is a knobbly root that grows underground.
- 🌸 Cloves are flower buds, dried in the sun.
- 🚢 Most spices cross an ocean by ship — a trip of many days.
- 🥧 Little bits of cold butter make a pie crust flaky.
- 🥚 Oven heat makes the eggs set — runny filling turns firm.

**People thanked**

## 🥂 Sparkling Grape Juice

*Adventure: Who puts the sparkle in the bottle?*

Source: your document “Sparkling Celebratory Drinks” (parts 2–7 in order; its closing aside on aging wine is an optional last room).

**What you do**

1. **The young vineyard** — test the soil, plant young vines, pull the trellis wire from post to post.
2. **Seasons of care** — prune in the snow, tuck spring shoots into the wires, shoo the starlings in summer.
3. **Harvest rush** — an “8 hours” clock starts: pick bunches, steer the harvester, truck the grapes to the press.
4. **The juice plant** — crush, press (skins and seeds stay behind), steam off some water, pasteurize with gentle heat.
5. **The bottling line** — water goes back in, add the bubbles, cap the bottles when the light is green, pack a carton.
6. **Store and home** — stock the shelf, buy a bottle, chill it, pop it, pour, toast.
7. **The wine cellar (optional)** — four things to look at: a weather wheel for vintages, the 55°F thermometer, bottles lying on their sides, a dusty bottle with a $2,000 tag. Nobody drinks; the toast upstairs is grape juice.

**Little games along the way** (you play them with the keys, the mouse or a touch screen; a wrong try only nudges you, and each one always ends in success)

- 🚜 **Drive the grape harvester** along the rows (the vineyard).
- 🍂 **Grapes in, leaves out!** *(new)* — things roll up a belt one at a time; send grapes IN to the crusher and leaves and twigs OUT (the juice plant).
- 🌡️ **Hold the pasteurizer** in the right range (the juice plant).
- 🍾 **Cap the bottles** as they pass (the bottling line).
- 💵 **Count out 4 dollars!** *(new)* — move bills from your wallet to the counter, take one back if you gave too many, then pay (the store).
- 🥂 **Stop at the line!** *(new)* — hold to pour each of the four glasses and let go in the green band; hold too long and the fizz spills over (home).

**“In real life” one-liners** (in the order they can appear)

- 🌍 Growers choose grape varieties that suit their climate and soil.
- 🌱 Young vines need strong roots before they can carry much fruit.
- ✂️ Pruning sleeping vines guides next season's growth.
- ☀️ Leaves capture sunlight, and the grapes slowly turn sweeter.
- ⏱️ Welch's: from vine to press within 8 hours of picking!
- 🚜 Some vineyards pick by hand; others use mechanical harvesters.
- 🍂 Leaves and stems come out before the grapes are crushed.
- 💧 Removing some water makes juice easier to store and transport.
- 🌡️ Pasteurizing: controlled heat reduces germs that could spoil the juice.
- 🍾 The fizz is carbon dioxide gas, squeezed in under pressure.
- 🔒 Bottles are sealed tight so the drink keeps its fizz.
- 🚚 Bottles travel through warehouses and delivery trucks to reach stores.
- ✨ Opening the bottle drops the pressure; the gas escapes as bubbles.
- 🥂 Pour slowly: less foam, and more fizz stays in the glass.
- 📅 Same vines, new weather — each harvest year's grapes differ.
- 🌡️ Cellars stay cool, dark and steady — about 55°F.
- 🍾 Cork-sealed bottles usually rest on their sides.
- 💰 Rare bottles can cost thousands — like rare paintings.

**People thanked**

- 🌱 Nursery workers, Growers, Planting crews, Trellis builders
- ✂️ Pruning crews, Vineyard workers, Agricultural specialists, Equipment mechanics
- 🍇 Growers, Picking crews, Harvester operators, Truck drivers
- 🏭 Press operators, Processing crews, Laboratory technicians, Sanitation workers
- 🍾 Food scientists, Bottling crews, Bottle makers, Label printers, Packaging workers
- 🛒 Warehouse crews, Truck drivers, Stockers, Cashiers
- 🥂 Whoever remembered the juice, Those serving at home, Many people we may never know

Closing line: “From a tiny vine to dancing bubbles — cheers to everyone who helped!”

## 🍽️ The China Plates

*Adventure: How is a china plate made?*

Source: your document “Thanksgiving Dinner – China”.

**What you do**

1. **China, long ago** — gather firewood and stoke a hillside kiln through day and night; hold the finished vase to the light; step through a gate in time.
2. **Meissen, Germany** — dig white kaolin with the two miners and ride the mine cart out.
3. **The clay works** — wash the clay, wait 6–8 weeks, mix in feldspar and quartz.
4. **New machines** — 1802: fire the steam boiler that turns the potter's wheel; today: fill the spray dryer and press a plate at 280 bar.
5. **Making it beautiful** — design your own plate pattern, print it as a transfer, press it onto your plate (your design stays on the plate to the end).
6. **Glaze and fire** — dip the plate in glaze, load the kiln, push the heat up to 1,400°C.

**Little games along the way** (you play them with the keys, the mouse or a touch screen; a wrong try only nudges you, and each one always ends in success)

- 🐉 **Feed the hungry hole!** *(new)* — the long dragon kiln has eight stoke holes up the hill; throw a log into the one that glows, bottom to top, and the fire climbs the kiln (China long ago).
- 🪨 **Tap the stones!** *(new)* — stones and clay lumps drift down the washing sluice; tap the stones out and let the clay float on (the clay works).
- ⏱️ **Press the plate** at the right moment (the factory).
- 🎨 **Design your own plate** (the design studio).
- 🎯 **Line up the print!** *(new)* — slide your printed design (drag, arrows or the buttons) until its dashed circle sits on the plate’s rim, then press it on (the design studio).

**“In real life” one-liners** (in the order they can appear)

- 🔥 Chinese makers found the secret: the right clay and very hot fire.
- 🐉 A dragon kiln is fed through side holes, bottom to top.
- 🏺 Porcelain was born in China, centuries before Europe made it.
- 🏰 1710: Meissen brings hard-paste porcelain making to Europe.
- ⚪ Kaolin, this pale clay, is what makes a plate white.
- 💧 Workers wash out everything that is not fine clay.
- ⚖️ Heavy sand and stones sink first; fine clay floats on.
- ⏳ Clay workers really do wait six to eight weeks for this!
- 💎 Real porcelain makers blend clay with feldspar, quartz and more.
- 🚂 In 1802, Spode used steam power to spin potters’ wheels.
- 🌀 Today some plates start as tiny spray-dried porcelain granules.
- 🖨️ Transfer printing, around 1750, made decorated plates cost much less.
- ☕ Bone china has real animal bone ash mixed in!
- 🛡️ Some decorations are fired into the glaze to protect them.
- 🌡️ Meissen fires its glaze at about 1,400°C — 2,550°F!

**People thanked**

- 🏺 Chinese potters, Kiln teams, Generations of inventors
- ⛏️ Miners, Clay washers, Material specialists
- ⚙️ Machine operators, Maintenance crews, Toolmakers
- 🎨 Designers, Model makers, Mold makers
- 🖨️ Engravers, Printers, Transfer decorators, Painters
- 🔥 Glazers, Kiln workers, Production artisans

Closing line: “From pale clay to a shining plate — look who helped!”

## 🧵 Grandma's Tablecloth

*Adventure: Where did Grandma's tablecloth come from?*

Source: your document “Grandma's Tablecloth”.

**What you do**

1. **1853, the flax field** — pull flax, soak it in the pond, break the stalks.
2. **Spin and weave** — spin thread, weave the cloth, bleach it on the grass, trade with the merchant.
3. **Anna's cottage** — light the candle, then stitch a flower yourself: satin-stitch petals, a stem-stitch vine, French knots.
4. **Across the Atlantic** — pack the trunk; in a storm at sea, tie down the sliding cargo.
5. **Across the plains** — walk beside the ox wagon gathering firewood; light the evening campfire.
6. **A home in Utah, through the generations** — the same room changes from the 1860s to the 1900s, 1930s, 1940s, 1960s and today as you lay the cloth, carry the wedding cake, set a place at the empty chair, choose which cloth stays, and wrap it in tissue.
7. **Thanksgiving Day, 2026** — lay the tablecloth; the grandchild asks where it came from.

**Little games along the way** (you play them with the keys, the mouse or a touch screen; a wrong try only nudges you, and each one always ends in success)

- ✨ **Comb and sort** *(new)* — say whether each combed bundle of flax is long (for linen) or short “tow” (for rope); six bundles (the flax field).
- 👟 **Treadle** *(new)* — the spinning wheel turns; tap when the dot reaches the star, six times, to wind the bobbin (spin and weave).
- 🧵 **Embroider the cloth** (the sewing room).
- 🐂 **Gee and haw** *(new)* — the teamster calls “Gee!” (right) or “Haw!” (left) at each bend of the trail; answer with the buttons or keys to turn the oxen (across the plains).
- 📦 **Chase the sliding cargo** in the storm (the ship).
- 🧶 **Choose the cloth** (the store).

**“In real life” one-liners** (in the order they can appear)

- 🌾 Flax is pulled, not cut — that keeps the fibres long.
- 💧 This soaking is called retting: tiny living things loosen the fibres.
- ✨ Combing sorts the fibre: long for linen, short "tow" for rope.
- 🧵 Weavers cross thousands of threads on a loom to make linen.
- ☀️ Water, sunlight and air bleach linen white, again and again.
- 🕯️ No electric light, no sewing machine — just needle and thread.
- 🌙 Some evenings Anna finished only a few inches.
- 🧳 Emigrants took only what they could carry — heavy furniture stayed behind.
- ⛈️ Storms made the long crossing frightening, and seasickness was common.
- 🐂 Teamsters walked beside the oxen, calling "gee" and "haw".
- 🔥 Pioneer families gathered fuel every day to cook over open fires.
- 🐂 Every pound mattered — yet the tablecloth came along.
- 🍲 In the Great Depression, the cloth showed beauty needs no money.
- 🛍️ By the 1960s, machine-made tablecloths were cheap and easy to find.
- 📦 Acid-free tissue and an archival box protect old linen.

**People thanked**

- 🌾 Flax farmers, Harvesters, Fibre processors
- 🧵 Spinners, Weavers, Merchants
- 🌸 Anna, Her skilled hands, Her patience
- ⛵ Sailors, Fellow emigrants, Anna's brave family
- 🐂 Wagon builders, Teamsters, Trail guides, Fellow pioneers
- 💛 Anna's daughter, Her granddaughter, Every generation since
- 📦 Family caretakers, Family storytellers, The next generation

Closing line: “Made by one woman — made possible by thousands.”

## 🌳 The Wooden Table

*Adventure: How does a tree become a table?*

No document from you for this one; you named “the table” in your message. Facts are kept simple and number-free.

**What you do**

1. **The oak forest** — count the rings on a stump, pull the two-person saw (“Timber!”), plant new oaks.
2. **The log landing** — load logs with the crane and ride the log truck.
3. **The sawmill** — strip the bark, saw boards, stack them, and tend the drying kiln.
4. **The workshop** — plane the boards, glue the top, fit the four legs, flip the table, sand, and oil it.
5. **Delivery day** — load the van with the driver and carry the table to the family's door.
6. **Home** — set the table down and spread Grandma's tablecloth over it.

**Little games along the way** (you play them with the keys, the mouse or a touch screen; a wrong try only nudges you, and each one always ends in success)

- 🌡️ **Keep the drying kiln** in the right range (the sawmill).
- ✋ **Rough first, smooth last!** *(new)* — three sandpapers (60, 120, 240); pick them in the order a finisher uses them, roughest to smoothest (the workshop).

**“In real life” one-liners** (in the order they can appear)

- ⚙️ Today, loggers mostly use chainsaws and big machines.
